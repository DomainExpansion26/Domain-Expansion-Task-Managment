## SECTION 1 — PROJECT IDENTITY & VISION

### 1.1 Project Name
**TechGuild** — a freelance platform architected on the Adventure Guild model from anime (DanMachi, Fairy Tail, Goblin Slayer, Solo Leveling, Re:Monster, Rising of the Shield Hero), adapted specifically for the technology industry.

### 1.2 One-Line Description
A ranking-based, party-driven freelance marketplace where solo freelancers, registered agencies, and verified clients transact through a tiered quest system that structurally enforces trust, mentorship, and career progression.

### 1.3 Vision Statement
TechGuild rebuilds freelance work as a structured professional career path rather than a transactional commodity market. By adapting the adventure guild archetype — ranked progression, party formation, quest-tier matching, mentorship hierarchies, and community guild halls — TechGuild addresses the structural failures of Upwork, Fiverr, and Freelancer.com that have suppressed freelancer sustainability, client outcomes, and marketplace quality for over a decade.

### 1.4 Core Thesis
Modern freelance platforms optimized for transaction throughput and platform take rate at the expense of craft progression, community durability, and trust verification. TechGuild inverts this priority order: trust verification and career progression come first, transaction volume follows as a natural consequence. The adventure guild model is not aesthetic theming — it is a proven thousand-year labor-organization system that solves exactly the problems freelance platforms have created.

### 1.5 Industry Context
The freelance platform market is effectively an oligopoly (Upwork, Fiverr, Freelancer.com controlling 70-80% of global volume) with documented structural defects cataloged across 80 specific problems in the companion document `problem-statement.md`. TechGuild is positioned as a structural alternative, not a feature-comparable competitor.

### 1.6 Co-Founder Roles
- **User (Visionary & Strategic Lead):** Original concept, product direction, market entry strategy, business priorities.
- **AI Co-Founder (Operations & Architecture Lead):** 15 years operating freelance agencies across Upwork, Fiverr, Freelancer.com. Top Rated Plus status sustained. Six-figure monthly billings at peak. Direct experience with platform suspensions, payment holds, JSS crashes, level reshuffles, policy massacres. Provides operational scar-tissue-based design input.

## SECTION 2 — CORE PHILOSOPHICAL FOUNDATION

### 2.1 The Five Philosophical Pillars

**Pillar 1 — Structured Progression Over Open Markets**
Free markets without structure devolve to race-to-bottom pricing. TechGuild enforces progression: F-rank entrants must earn the right to access larger projects through verified work, not just bid aggressively.

**Pillar 2 — Collaboration Over Isolation**
Solo freelancing is structurally isolating and creates burnout, mentorship gaps, and execution risk. TechGuild forces party formation as a rank-up prerequisite, making collaboration a condition of career advancement.

**Pillar 3 — Verified Trust Over Claimed Trust**
Claims of skill, portfolio, and identity are unverifiable on existing platforms. TechGuild uses AI-verified work products, feedback verification, and on-chain credentialing to make trust verifiable rather than asserted.

**Pillar 4 — Tier-Matched Quests Over Open Bidding**
Clients should not receive 80 generic proposals; parties should not bid on projects outside their verified capability tier. TechGuild matches project budget to party rank, structurally preventing the proposal flood that has degraded every existing platform.

**Pillar 5 — Community As Infrastructure, Not Feature**
Mentorship, knowledge sharing, joint ventures, and peer networks are not add-on features — they are the platform's core infrastructure. The Guild Hall (community space) is as central as the Quest Board (project marketplace).

### 2.2 Design Principles
- Every feature must reduce a documented problem from `problem-statement.md`. No feature is built for feature-paragraphity.
- Anime terminology is used internally and as user-facing branding where it enhances clarity. It is never used to obscure functionality.
- Verification burden is accepted as a cost of trust. We will not optimize for low-friction onboarding at the expense of trust integrity.
- The platform is a labor-organization system, not a marketplace. The distinction matters: marketplaces optimize for transactions; labor organizations optimize for participants.

## SECTION 3 — RANKING SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

### 3.1 The Nine-Tier Rank Ladder

TechGuild uses a nine-tier ranking system adapted from adventure guild hierarchies. Ranks apply to three entity types: **Individual Freelancers**, **Parties** (groups of 5+ freelancers), and **Registered Agencies** (formal business entities).

| Rank | Name | Anime Equivalent | Description |
|------|------|------------------|-------------|
| F | Aspirant | Page / Squire | Entry tier. All new freelancers, all new agencies, all new parties start here. No prerequisite to enter. |
| E | Initiate | Apprentice | First progression tier. Requires party formation (for individuals) or registered agency status. |
| D | Novice | Junior Adventurer | Established party/agency with verified track record of small-project completion. |
| C | Adept | Adventurer | Mid-tier with portfolio of completed projects across multiple client types. |
| B | Skilled | Senior Adventurer | Strong track record, peer-recognized quality, eligible for medium-budget projects. |
| A | Expert | Elite Adventurer | Top-tier verified quality, eligible for enterprise-tier projects. |
| S | Master | S-Rank Adventurer | Industry-recognized excellence, eligible for the highest-value projects. |
| SS | Grandmaster | Hero / Champion | Rare tier reserved for exceptional sustained track record over multiple years. |
| SSS | Legendary | Guild Master / Legend | Highest attainable rank. Reserved for parties/agencies with multi-year S-rank performance and significant community contribution. |

### 3.2 Rank Progression Rules

**F → E (The Party Formation Gate):**
- Individual freelancers can register at F rank freely.
- To progress from F to E, an individual freelancer must do ONE of the following:
  1. **Form a Party:** Join or create a group of at least 5 individual freelancers with a chosen group name. The party becomes a collective entity.
  2. **Register as an Agency:** Provide business registration documents (PAN/GST in India, equivalent in other jurisdictions).
  3. **Join an Existing Registered Agency:** Be added as a contractor under an agency's TechGuild account.
- Registered agencies and existing businesses can enter directly at E rank upon verification of business documents.

**E → D and Upward:**
- Only parties and registered agencies can progress from E upward. Solo individuals remain capped at E rank (pending design decision — see Section 12, Open Question 7).
- Rank-up criteria include: number of completed projects, genuine verified feedback, peer endorsements, and additional criteria still being defined (see Section 10).

**Downward Movement:**
- Ranks can be lost through sustained poor performance, verified fraud, or platform-side review (criteria TBD — see Section 12, Open Question 1).

### 3.3 Rank Visibility & Authority
- Higher ranks have access to higher-budget project tiers (see Section 5).
- Higher ranks receive premium platform features: priority support, lower platform fees (TBD), Guild Hall moderation privileges, mentorship eligibility.
- S, SS, and SSS ranks carry public-facing badges and may be referenced as professional credentials outside the platform.

### 3.4 Rank vs. Reputation — The Per-Quest Reputation Layer
Beyond the global rank (F-SSS), parties and individuals also accumulate **per-skill reputation**. A party may be "S-Rank in Frontend Quests" while being "D-Rank in DevOps Quests." This addresses Problem 11 (JSS manipulation) and Problem 26 (industry-specific knowledge gaps) by making reputation granular and verifiable per skill domain rather than aggregated into a single opaque score.

Per-quest reputation is tracked publicly on the party's profile and forms part of the rank-up evaluation. A party with S-Rank reputation in 5+ skill domains is a strong candidate for global S-rank promotion.

## SECTION 4 — PARTY SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

### 4.1 What Is a Party?
A **Party** is a collective of 5 or more individual freelancers who have formally associated on TechGuild to bid on and execute projects together. The party is the primary operating unit of TechGuild — solo freelancers are accepted but structurally limited in advancement until they form or join a party.

### 4.2 Party Formation Mechanics
- **Minimum size:** 5 members
- **Required at formation:** A chosen party name (the "Guild Name" equivalent)
- **Composition flexibility:** Parties can be skill-homogeneous (all frontend developers) or skill-diverse (frontend + backend + designer + PM + QA). Diverse parties are more competitive for multi-skill quests.
- **Internal governance:** Parties self-organize. TechGuild does not impose internal hierarchy, but recommends roles (Party Lead, etc.) for client-facing accountability.
- **Legal status:** A party is initially a platform-side grouping, NOT a legal entity. However, parties that grow to B+ rank are encouraged (and may be required, pending decision) to register as a legal business entity to access higher-tier projects.

### 4.3 Party Lifecycle
- **Formation:** 5+ freelancers agree to associate, choose a name, submit formation request to TechGuild.
- **Verification:** TechGuild verifies party members are real, distinct individuals (not multi-accounting — addresses Problem 10). Verification method TBD (see Section 11).
- **Operation:** Party bids on projects collectively. Work is distributed among members internally. Client sees the party as a single unit; individual member contributions are tracked internally.
- **Member Changes:** Members can leave; new members can join. Member departure does not dissolve the party as long as minimum size is maintained. Reputation earned during a member's tenure stays with the party (pending decision — see Section 12, Open Question 2).
- **Dissolution:** Party can be dissolved by member vote or platform-side action. Reputation of a dissolved party is forfeited (prevents reputation laundering).

### 4.4 Party Reputation & Liability
- Reputation accrues to the party, not to individual members. This is the adventure guild model — the guild's name is what clients trust.
- If a party member ghosts on a project, the remaining members are responsible for completion. This addresses Problem 33 (mid-project abandonment) structurally — clients are never stranded because the party persists.
- If a party member commits verified fraud, the entire party's rank is impacted. This is "common responsibility" — the adventure guild model where one member's failure is the guild's failure. This creates internal policing incentives.
- Open question: legal exposure of common responsibility (see Section 12, Open Question 2).

### 4.5 Why Force Party Formation?
The mandatory party formation for F → E progression is the single most important structural decision in TechGuild. It simultaneously addresses:
- Problem 33 (mid-project abandonment) — party absorbs ghosting
- Problem 28 (scope creep) — party distributes scope
- Problem 45 (race-to-bottom pricing) — party commands higher collective rates
- Problem 61 (no mentorship infrastructure) — party creates natural mentorship
- Problem 62 (isolation) — party provides daily professional community
- Problem 63 (career stagnation) — party progression is the career path
- Startup formation catalyst — parties often formalize into registered companies

This is not a feature. This is the load-bearing wall of the entire platform.

## SECTION 5 — PROJECT/QUEST DISTRIBUTION MECHANICS

### 5.1 The Budget-Tier Locking System
TechGuild's central marketplace mechanic: **project visibility is locked by budget tier and matched to party rank.** A client posting a project at a given budget only sees (and is only seen by) parties of the rank appropriate to that budget.

### 5.2 The Tier Matrix (Illustrative — Currency Strategy Pending)

Based on INR-denominated example provided by user:

| Project Budget (INR) | Visible To Ranks |
|----------------------|------------------|
| Up to ₹10,000 | F rank only |
| ₹10,000 – ₹40,000 | E and F rank |
| ₹40,000+ | D rank and above |
| Higher tiers (TBD) | C, B, A, S, SS, SSS |
| Enterprise tier (TBD) | S, SS, SSS only |

**Note:** The exact tier breakpoints for C through SSS ranks are still being designed. The principle is established: as project budget increases, the pool of eligible parties narrows to higher ranks.

### 5.3 Locking Type (Open Design Decision)
Three models are on the table:
- **Hard Lock:** F rank literally cannot see or bid on a ₹40K+ project. Strict, clean, but may trap clients who can't find a D+ available.
- **Soft Lock:** D+ gets exclusive first 48-72 hours; then opens to lower ranks with platform-side warning. More flexible, more complex.
- **Client Choice:** Default filtered to D+, but client can opt to open downward. Maximum flexibility, weakest protection.

**Co-founder AI recommendation:** Soft lock with 72-hour D+ exclusive window, then opens downward with platform-side warning.
**User position:** TBD — pending discussion.

### 5.4 Why This Works
Tier-locked project distribution simultaneously solves:
- Problem 1 (ghosting after partial payment) — small projects at F rank contain fraud blast radius
- Problem 16 (old account visibility monopoly) — rank-based, not age-based visibility
- Problem 18 (cold start) — F rank gets F-appropriate projects, not losing to veterans
- Problem 34 (spam bid overwhelm) — clients see only tier-appropriate parties
- Problem 35 (generic proposal flood) — fewer, more qualified proposals per client
- Problem 45 (race-to-bottom) — pricing floor established by tier structure

### 5.5 Emergency Quest Tier
A specialized project type adapted from anime emergency quest mechanics: urgent client needs (production outages, launch-critical bugs, time-sensitive deliverables) are flagged as Emergency Quests with:
- Premium pricing (platform-suggested markup)
- Visibility expanded to all qualifying ranks simultaneously
- Reputation boost for successful completion (counts double toward rank-up)
- Penalty for failure (reputation deduction, possible rank review)

This addresses Problem 33 (mid-project abandonment for urgent work) and creates a premium market segment for high-capability parties.

## SECTION 6 — CLIENT RANKING SYSTEM

### 6.1 Confirmed: Clients Are Also Ranked
The user has confirmed that clients will also receive ranks. This is a significant design decision that inverts the Upwork/Fiverr model where clients are unranked and effectively unaccountable.

### 6.2 Client Rank Tiers (Tentative)
Same nine-tier ladder (F through SSS) applies to clients. Entry at F. Progression based on:
- Total project volume funded
- Average project budget
- Payment speed and reliability
- Feedback quality given to parties (genuine vs. retaliatory)
- Dispute history (initiated disputes, dispute outcomes)
- Repeat engagement with same parties (loyalty signal)

### 6.3 Client Rank Effects (Design Pending)
Open questions for client ranking:
- Does client rank affect which parties see their projects? (User suggested yes — high-rank clients see high-rank parties only, low-rank clients see low-rank parties only.)
- Does client rank affect platform fees? (TBD)
- Does client rank affect support priority? (TBD)
- Can clients improve their rank over time, or are past disputes permanent? (TBD)

### 6.4 Why Client Ranking Matters
Client ranking directly addresses:
- Problem 12 (client review extortion) — bad clients lose visibility to good parties
- Problem 13 (review bombing) — coordinated attacks become visible in client rank
- Problem 2 (escrow manipulation by clients) — repeat offenders drop in rank
- Problem 41 (selective enforcement) — enforcement becomes algorithmic, not discretionary

The core insight: **if bad clients become invisible to good parties, bad client behavior has structural consequences.** This is the single most powerful anti-abuse mechanism available, and no existing platform implements it.

### 6.5 The Two-Sided Locking Effect
Combined with Section 5's budget-tier locking, the platform creates a two-sided matching system:
- High-rank clients → see high-rank parties only → transact at high budgets → maintain rank through quality engagement
- Low-rank clients → see low-rank parties only → transact at low budgets → must improve behavior to access better parties

This is the structural answer to Problems 12, 13, 41, and several others. It must be implemented carefully to avoid trapping well-intentioned clients in a low-rank prison from which they cannot escape.

## SECTION 8 — AI INTEGRATION STRATEGY

### 8.1 Confirmed AI Application Areas

**AI Application 1 — Feedback Verification**
When a party/agency receives feedback (positive or negative), TechGuild's AI contacts the feedback giver directly to verify the feedback is genuine and not fabricated. This directly addresses:
- Problem 69 (review authenticity erosion)
- Problem 13 (review bombing)
- Problem 12 (client review extortion — fake positive feedback for refunds)
- Problem 20 (SEO review trading)

Verification mechanism: AI-driven conversation with the feedback giver, asking context-specific questions only the genuine client could answer (project details, deliverable specifics, communication history). Suspected fake feedback is flagged for human review and excluded from rank calculations pending verification.

**AI Application 2 — Social Media Content Verification**
Posts to the Guild Hall social platform (Section 9) are AI-verified for genuineness before publication. AI checks:
- Claimed case study details match platform transaction records
- Images are not stolen from other sources (reverse image search + AI content detection)
- Posted metrics (revenue, project size, client names) align with verified platform data
- Content is not promotional spam, fraud, or off-topic

Genuine content is published; flagged content is held for human review or rejected.

**AI Application 3 — Skill Assessment Verification (Guild Card)**
AI-administered skill assessments for the Guild Card (Section 7.1). Assessments are dynamic (AI generates unique questions per assessment to prevent answer-key leakage), multi-modal (code, design, written), and produce verifiable credentials. This addresses Problem 23 (skill test gaming) and Problem 24 (no verifiable credentialing).

**AI Application 4 — Matchmaking & Quest Distribution**
AI-driven matching of clients to parties based on:
- Skill requirements vs. party per-quest reputation
- Budget tier vs. party rank
- Client rank vs. party rank (two-sided matching)
- Historical performance on similar quest types
- Availability and capacity signals

### 8.2 Additional AI Application Areas (Co-Founder AI Recommendations, Pending User Decision)
The following AI applications are recommended by the AI co-founder based on operational experience but have not yet been confirmed by the user:
- **AI dispute triage** — initial dispute classification and evidence assessment before human review
- **AI scope-creep detection** — monitoring contract communication for scope expansion beyond original brief
- **AI fraud pattern detection** — multi-account farming, portfolio theft, bid flooding
- **AI soft-skill assessment** — analyzing party communication patterns to surface soft-skill quality (addresses Problem 27)
- **AI tax/compliance assistance** — automated VAT/GST determination for cross-border transactions (addresses Problem 60)

### 8.3 AI Verification Philosophy
TechGuild's AI is positioned as a **verification layer**, not a **decision maker**. AI flags, classifies, and recommends; humans (platform staff or designated senior community members) make final decisions on rank changes, suspensions, and content publication. This avoids the failure mode of platform-side algorithmic punishment without recourse (Problem 15, Problem 17).

## SECTION 9 — GUILD HALL SOCIAL PLATFORM

### 9.1 Concept
The Guild Hall is TechGuild's integrated social media platform — accessible to all registered parties, agencies, and clients. It serves as:
- A community space for professional updates and case study sharing
- A knowledge-sharing layer (addressing Problem 64 — knowledge silos)
- A discovery layer (parties find collaborators; clients find parties through demonstrated work)
- A reputation layer (public case studies reinforce rank credibility)

### 9.2 Content Types
- **Case Studies:** Parties post detailed breakdowns of completed projects (problem, approach, solution, outcome). AI-verified against platform transaction records.
- **Project Updates:** Real-time updates during active project execution (with client opt-in).
- **Knowledge Posts:** Technical articles, lessons learned, methodology breakdowns.
- **Party/Agency Introductions:** New parties introduce themselves; existing parties announce member changes.
- **Client Posts:** Clients post about their businesses, project needs, and feedback on completed work.
- **Community Discussions:** Open threads on industry topics, best practices, market trends.

### 9.3 AI Verification Pipeline
All posts pass through AI verification before publication:
1. **Claim Verification:** Any factual claim (project size, client name, deliverable metrics) is checked against platform records.
2. **Content Authenticity:** Images checked for theft/AI-generation; text checked for plagiarism and AI-generated fraud.
3. **Relevance Check:** Posts must be on-topic for the Guild Hall's professional context. Spam and off-topic content rejected.
4. **Quality Threshold:** AI evaluates post quality (depth, clarity, usefulness). Low-quality posts may be published with reduced visibility rather than rejected.
5. **Human Review Queue:** Borderline cases route to human moderators (initially platform staff, eventually senior community members).

### 9.4 Why This Matters
The Guild Hall addresses:
- Problem 61 (no mentorship) — knowledge posts create distributed mentorship
- Problem 62 (isolation) — daily community contact
- Problem 64 (knowledge silos) — institutional memory captured and shared
- Problem 24 (no verifiable credentialing) — case studies become verifiable proof of work
- Problem 25 (AI-generated portfolio flooding) — AI verification filters fake portfolios at publication

### 9.5 Discovery & Network Effects
The Guild Hall is also TechGuild's discovery engine. Clients browsing the Guild Hall see verified case studies from parties they might never have encountered through search alone. Parties browsing see collaboration opportunities and industry trends. The social layer creates network effects that compound with the marketplace layer — every additional active party increases the value of the Guild Hall, which increases party acquisition, which increases marketplace liquidity.

## SECTION 10 — RANK-UP CRITERIA (PARTIAL DESIGN)

### 10.1 Confirmed Signals
The user has confirmed rank-up will be based on:
1. **Number of completed projects** (volume signal)
2. **Genuine verified feedback** (quality signal — AI-verified per Section 8.1)
3. **Additional criteria "like in the adventure guild but related to tech industry only"** (specific criteria TBD)

### 10.2 Co-Founder AI Recommendations for Additional Criteria
Based on operational experience and the problem statement, the AI co-founder recommends the following additional rank-up signals (pending user confirmation):

**Recommended Additional Signals:**
- **Per-quest reputation scores** (Section 3.4) — S-Rank reputation in N skill domains required for global S-rank promotion
- **Client retention rate** — percentage of clients who re-engage the party for additional work
- **Project completion rate** — percentage of accepted projects delivered without abandonment
- **Peer endorsements** — verified endorsements from other B+ rank parties (collusion-resistant through rank threshold)
- **Guild Hall contributions** — published case studies, knowledge posts, mentorship activity
- **Emergency Quest success rate** — performance under high-pressure conditions
- **Time-in-rank** — minimum tenure at each rank before promotion eligibility (prevents rapid gaming)
- **Dispute resolution outcome** — clean dispute record vs. pattern of disputes

### 10.3 Explicitly Rejected Signals
To avoid recreating Upwork/Fiverr failure modes, the following signals will NOT be used as primary rank-up criteria:
- Account age (addresses Problem 16 — old account visibility monopoly)
- Total revenue (addresses Problem 45 — race-to-bottom; revenue alone doesn't indicate quality)
- Hourly rate (gameable and not quality-correlated)
- Connects purchased / bid boost spending (addresses Problem 57 — pay-to-win)
- Self-declared skill tags (addresses Problem 20 — SEO keyword stuffing)

### 10.4 The Hybrid Model
The rank-up formula will be hybrid (multiple weighted signals) rather than single-signal. Exact weightings are pending design and will be documented in the plan file. The principle: no single signal should be gameable enough to dominate the formula. Multiple weak signals, combined, produce a strong composite signal that is significantly harder to manipulate than any single metric.

### 10.5 Transparency
The exact rank-up formula will be partially transparent (users see which signals contribute and approximately how to improve) but not fully transparent (specific weightings hidden to prevent gaming). This balances Problem 15 (algorithm opacity — users deserve to understand the system) against the gaming risk that full transparency creates.

## SECTION 11 — VERIFICATION & TRUST FRAMEWORK

### 11.1 The Verification Burden
TechGuild's verification needs are MORE complex than existing platforms, not less. We must verify:
- Individual freelancer identity (Problem 9 — existing platforms fail this 8-15% of the time)
- Party member distinctness (Problem 10 — multi-account farming)
- Business registration authenticity (for registered agencies)
- Skill claims through AI assessments (Guild Card)
- Work product authenticity (portfolio verification)
- Feedback authenticity (AI verification per Section 8.1)
- Social media post authenticity (AI verification per Section 9.3)

### 11.2 Verification Methods (Multi-Layer)
- **Layer 1 — Document Verification:** Government IDs, business registration documents, tax IDs. Third-party services (Onfido, Persona, etc.) with manual fallback for edge cases.
- **Layer 2 — Behavioral Verification:** Activity patterns, communication style consistency, project execution patterns. AI-driven anomaly detection.
- **Layer 3 — Network Verification:** Cross-references between party members, clients, and platform history to detect multi-accounting and collusion rings.
- **Layer 4 — AI Verification:** Per Sections 8.1, 8.2, 9.3 — AI-driven verification of feedback, social posts, and skill assessments.
- **Layer 5 — Human Verification:** Senior community members (B+ rank) serve as verification deputies for borderline cases, escalations, and appeals. This distributes verification burden and creates community ownership of trust.

### 11.3 Verification Failure Handling
- Legitimate users who fail verification (Problem 9 on existing platforms) must have a clear, fast, human-reviewed appeal path.
- Verification failures do not result in immediate account suspension; they result in restricted functionality pending resolution.
- Users in regions with weak document infrastructure (Problem 9 geographic bias) have alternative verification paths (peer endorsement, video verification, work history verification).

### 11.4 Cross-Platform Reputation Import (Open Question)
TechGuild may allow new entrants to import reputation from Upwork, Fiverr, Freelancer.com through verified API integrations or document submission. This would address Problem 14 (non-portable reputation) directly. Open question for user decision (see Section 12, Open Question 6).

## SECTION 12 — OPEN DESIGN QUESTIONS

These are the unresolved design decisions identified during brainstorming. Each must be resolved before or during plan file creation.

### Q1: Rank-Down and Suspension Criteria
**Question:** What specific behaviors trigger rank demotion or account suspension? How is this different from Upwork's opaque suspension regime (Problem 38)?
**Status:** Open
**Co-founder AI position:** Transparent published criteria, with human review before any demotion/suspension takes effect. Three-strike system for minor violations; immediate suspension only for verified fraud.

### Q2: Party Member Liability & Reputation Portability
**Question:** If a party member leaves, do they take any reputation with them? Is the party legally liable for individual member actions?
**Status:** Open
**Co-founder AI position:** Reputation stays with the party. Members who leave start fresh at their new party (or as solo F-rank). Legal liability is individual, not collective, but platform-side rank consequences are collective.

### Q3: F-Rank Protection Mechanism
**Question:** How do we prevent F rank from becoming a graveyard where new entrants get burned and churn before reaching E?
**Status:** Open
**Co-founder AI position:** F-rank-specific protections: mandatory escrow, platform-side QA on all F-rank deliverables, reduced platform fees at F rank, free mentorship pairing with B+ rank parties.

### Q4: Client Rank Escape Valve
**Question:** If a low-rank client improves their behavior, can they climb? Or are past disputes permanent?
**Status:** Open
**Co-founder AI position:** Client rank is rolling (last 12-24 months only). Past disputes age out. Improvement is possible and rewarded.

### Q5: Tier-Lock Type (Hard / Soft / Hybrid)
**Question:** Are project-tier locks hard, soft, or client-choice?
**Status:** Open
**Co-founder AI position:** Soft lock with 72-hour D+ exclusive window, then opens downward with platform-side warning.

### Q6: Currency & Geographic Strategy
**Question:** Is TechGuild India-first launch, global-day-one, or phased? How are INR/USD/EUR tiers reconciled?
**Status:** Open
**User indicated:** INR-denominated example provided, suggesting India-first consideration.

### Q7: Solo Specialist Track
**Question:** Is there a path for individual freelancers (no party) to progress beyond E rank? Or is party formation strictly mandatory for all advancement?
**Status:** Open
**Co-founder AI position:** Allow solo specialists to progress to B rank maximum. A rank and above require party/agency structure. This respects solo operators while preserving the structural benefit of party formation for top-tier work.

### Q8: Verification Operations Model
**Question:** Heavy human verification (expensive, slow, accurate) vs. light algorithmic verification (cheap, fast, gameable)? Or hybrid?

### Decision 19 — Multi-Layer Contribution Verification System
**Date:** 2026-06-23
**Decision:** TechGuild implements a 6-layer contribution verification system to address the gap between task completion status (meaningless) and actual work quality (what matters). All 6 layers apply to every quest deliverable:

**Layer 1: AI Originality Check (Mandatory, Every Deliverable)**
- Every submitted deliverable scanned by AI for plagiarism, AI-generation, copy-paste
- Code: compared against GitHub corpus + Stack Overflow patterns
- Text: compared against web content + AI-generation detection
- Design: reverse image search + AI-generation detection
- Flagged deliverables → human review before milestone approval

**Layer 2: Client Milestone Approval (Mandatory)**
- Client must explicitly approve each milestone with "this work is acceptable and meets scope"
- Auto-approval only after 14 days IF client has been active (login, viewed submission)
- Client-side rating per milestone (not just star rating — "did this deliverable meet expectations?")

**Layer 3: Peer Review Within Party (Mandatory for Multi-Member Work)**
- Before milestone submission, at least 1 other party member must review the work
- Reviewer signs off: "I reviewed this work; it meets quality standards"
- Reviewer's reputation tied to review quality (bad reviews = reviewer loses review privilege)

**Layer 4: Work-In-Progress Check-Ins (Mandatory for Quests >2 Weeks)**
- For longer quests, members must share work-in-progress at periodic checkpoints (every 3-5 days)
- Proves ongoing engagement, not last-minute copy-paste
- AI checks WIP snapshots for incremental progress

**Layer 5: Live Demonstration (For Major Milestones >₹50K)**
- For high-value milestones, member who did the work must demo it live to Party Lead or client
- 15-30 minute screen-share session
- Demo recorded for dispute resolution
- Catches "I copied this but don't understand it" pattern

**Layer 6: AI Contribution Analysis (Continuous)**
- AI analyzes deliverable vs. assigned task scope
- "This task required custom React component; submission contains 90% boilerplate from public templates" = flag
- Pattern detection across multiple tasks from same member

**Rationale:** User identified critical gap — task completion status is meaningless if work is copy-pasted. After 15 years operating agencies, AI Co-Founder confirms this is the hardest verification problem. No single check solves it; multi-layer verification is the only answer. This system addresses L-07 (Shell Parties with Accomplices), L-61 (Free-Rider Problem), L-63 (Contribution Manipulation), and several AI verification loopholes.
**Status:** Confirmed. Implementation Phase: Phases 2-3 (basic layers in Phase 2 with party system; advanced AI layers in Phase 3 with AI services). Cost/Effort: Very High (continuous AI analysis + reviewer reputation system + demo recording infrastructure).

### Decision 20 — Party Lead Role with Bounded Authority
**Date:** 2026-06-23
**Decision:** TechGuild formalizes the Party Lead role — the member who creates the party is initial Lead. Lead has operational authority but with structural limits to prevent abuse (per L-08 and L-63 mitigations already designed).

**Lead Authority (What Lead Can Do):**
- Accept quests on behalf of party
- Declare initial splits (subject to member approval before work begins)
- Be primary point of contact for client
- Make day-to-day operational decisions
- Coordinate party members during quests

**Lead Authority Limits (What Lead Cannot Do):**

- Whoever creates the party is initial Lead
- Lead can transfer Lead role to another member (with 60% member approval)

- If party drops below 5 members, 30-day grace period to recruit before auto-demotion

**Rationale:** User asked whether Party Lead role should exist. AI Co-Founder confirms YES — operational necessity (someone must coordinate) — but with bounded authority to prevent L-08 (Lead expulsion abuse) and L-63 (Contribution manipulation). Bounded Lead model is the standard for collaborative organizations (CEO + board structure; captain + team vote structure; etc.).
**Status:** Confirmed. Implementation Phase: Phase 2 (party system). Cost/Effort: Medium (voting system + audit log + arbitration workflow).

**Layer 1: Quick Start Guide (Mandatory at Signup, 5-minute read)**

**Rationale:** User directed — show all platform rules, TechGuild rules, party rules, and policy violation warnings clearly. Consequences (derank or ban) must be transparent. ### Decision 23 — Universal Govt ID Verification Strengthened

**What This Solves:**

- L-28 (Verification Expiry & Account Sale) — new "owner" can't re-verify with same ID

### Decision 24 — Party Creation & Membership Rules
**Date:** 2026-06-23
**Decision:** TechGuild enforces structural limits on party creation and membership to prevent party-hopping, shell party farms, and reputation laundering.

**Membership Rules:**
- Each freelancer can be a member of AT MOST ONE party at any time
- Cannot be in a party AND registered as agency simultaneously (one or the other)
- Freelancers can take individual F-rank quests while in a party (subject to Decision 26 — Conditional Individual Work)

**Creation Rules:**
- Each freelancer can CREATE at most ONE party per calendar year (Jan 1 – Dec 31) for free
- If a freelancer has already created a party this year, additional party creations cost ₹5,000 INR each
- The creation fee applies even if the previous party was dissolved (prevents L-02 — Dissolution & Reform)
- The fee is friction, not punishment
- Joiners (members who didn't create the party) can create their first party for free (haven't used their "creation slot")

**Rationale:** User proposed these rules to address L-02 (Party Dissolution & Reform) and L-07 (Shell Parties). "One party at a time" prevents someone from being in 5 shell parties simultaneously. "One creation per year + ₹5K fee" makes reputation laundering expensive — the bad actor who wants to dissolve "Phoenix Guild" and reform as "Phoenix Reborn" now faces ₹5K friction each time. Combined with Decision 18 (parameter carry-over limits), this makes party dissolution economically painful. Joiners can create first party for free — fair because they haven't used their creation slot, prevents people being trapped in bad parties because they can't afford to create their own.
**Status:** Confirmed. Implementation Phase: Phase 2 (party system). Cost/Effort: Low (validation logic + fee collection + calendar tracking).

### Decision 25 — Distribution Approval Protocol (Refined from L-61)
**Date:** 2026-06-23
**Decision:** Refines L-61 (Pre-Quest Split Declaration) with explicit approval workflow, counter-proposal mechanism, and deadlock-breaking rules.

**Protocol:**
1. Party Lead proposes initial distribution at quest acceptance
2. All members must approve distribution before work begins
3. Approval threshold: 60% of party members (consistent with Decision 20 — Lead replacement vote)
4. Members can submit COUNTER-PROPOSAL within 48 hours of Lead's proposal
5. If counter-proposal gets 60% approval, it replaces Lead's proposal
6. AI can suggest fair distribution based on past contribution data from similar quests (advisory, not binding)
7. After 3 failed attempts at 60% approval, quest cannot be accepted by this party (prevents infinite loop)
8. Once approved, distribution is LOCKED — cannot be changed without all-member re-approval
9. If member leaves mid-quest, L-65 pro-rated payment protocol applies

**Rationale:** User independently arrived at the same "Lead proposes, members approve" principle as L-61, validating the design. AI Co-Founder refinements: counter-proposal mechanism prevents "take it or leave it" power plays by Lead; 3-failure deadlock break prevents both Lead intransigence AND member obstruction; AI suggestion provides neutral baseline. User accepted all refinements.
**Status:** Confirmed. Implementation Phase: Phase 2 (party system + finance). Cost/Effort: Medium (approval workflow + counter-proposal UI + deadlock detection). Replaces the simpler "Lead declares, members approve" model from L-61 Layer 1.

### Decision 26 — Conditional Individual Work Rules
**Date:** 2026-06-23
**Decision:** Party members can take individual F-rank quests for additional income, BUT only when their party has no active quest in progress. This is a conditional restriction, not a blanket ban.

**Rules:**
- If party has NO active quest (between projects) → members CAN take individual F-rank quests for cash flow
- If party has active quest (quest in progress) → members CANNOT take individual F-rank quests simultaneously
- Members must focus on party work when party has active commitments
- This applies per-member (each member's individual work is gated by party's active status)
- Individual F-rank work is logged separately from party work for reputation tracking

**Rationale:** User refined AI Co-Founder's "freelancers can take individual F-rank quests while in a party" recommendation with a sensible condition — only when party has no active project. This balances two needs: (1) cash flow for party members during slow periods (party work may be sporadic); (2) focus and commitment when party has active client work. Without this condition, members might neglect party work for individual work, undermining party reliability. With this condition, members can supplement income during gaps but must commit to party when party has work.
**Status:** Confirmed. Implementation Phase: Phase 2 (party system + quest board). Cost/Effort: Low (status check + eligibility filter).

### Decision 27 — Asymmetric Tier Visibility
**Date:** 2026-06-23
**Decision:** Refines Decision 4 (Tier-Locked Project Distribution) from symmetric lock to asymmetric lock. Higher-ranking entities can see and access lower-tier projects; lower-ranking entities cannot see higher-tier projects.

**Visibility Rules:**
- Higher-rank parties/agencies CAN see and bid on lower-budget projects (downward visibility allowed)
- Lower-rank parties/agencies CANNOT see higher-budget projects (upward visibility blocked)
- Same principle applies to clients: higher-rank clients can see lower-rank parties; lower-rank clients cannot see higher-rank parties
- F-rank sees ONLY F-tier projects (₹0-₹10K range)
- E-rank sees F + E tier projects
- D-rank sees F + E + D tier projects
- ...and so on up to SSS rank which sees ALL projects

**Conditions (AI Co-Founder refinements):**
1. "Best match" algorithm prioritizes fit + rank together (not rank alone) — F-rank parties with great fit can still win against higher-rank competition
2. Higher-rank parties accepting lower-tier quests get a "Portfolio/Mentoring Quest" badge visible to clients (transparency about why they're taking lower-tier work)

**Rationale:** User proposed asymmetric tier visibility, which aligns with the anime adventure guild model (in DanMachi, S-rank adventurers CAN take F-rank quests; restriction is upward, not downward). This rewards higher-rank parties with broader access and trusts the market to self-regulate — S-rank parties charge S-rank rates, so they won't typically bid on ₹500 quests (not worth their time). The two AI Co-Founder conditions mitigate the cold-start pressure (F-rank can still win via fit-based matching) and cherry-picking risk (transparency badge makes it clear when S-rank is taking low-tier work for portfolio reasons).
**Status:** Confirmed. Implementation Phase: Phase 2 (quest board) — supersedes the symmetric lock from Decision 4. Cost/Effort: Medium (visibility logic + best-match algorithm + badge system). Note: This decision MODIFIES Decision 4 — the symmetric lock is replaced with asymmetric lock.

### Decision 28 — Client Complaint System (3-Strike Progressive)
**Date:** 2026-06-23
**Decision:** TechGuild implements a 3-stage progressive complaint system that allows clients to make complaints against parties/agencies/freelancers for ghosting, faulty work, wrong work, or other issues. The system uses AI as a neutral warning layer to prevent both party abuse and client weaponization.

**Stage 1: First Complaint — Warning + AI Action**
- Client submits complaint with evidence (screenshots, description, category)
- AI evaluates complaint legitimacy (frivolous → rejected, counts against client reputation)
- If legitimate: AI sends specific actionable warning to party/freelancer with deadline
- Warning logged in party's internal record (not public)
- Party has 24-48 hours to respond/fix
- If party fixes issue → complaint resolved, no rank impact
- If party doesn't respond → complaint escalates to Stage 2 automatically

**Stage 2: Second Complaint (Same Type within 30 days) — Cancellation Eligibility**
- Client submits second complaint of SAME TYPE within 30 days
- Client becomes eligible to cancel project with full refund (minus work already completed)
- AI notifies party: "Second complaint received. Client may cancel. Resolve immediately."
- Cancellation is NOT automatic — client chooses to cancel or continue
- If party resolves issue → client can withdraw cancellation request
- If client cancels → party's rank impacted, dispute record created

**Stage 3: Third Complaint (Same Type within 90 days) — Forced Cancellation + Rank Review**
- Third complaint of same type within 90 days
- Platform FORCES project cancellation
- Full refund to client for uncompleted work
- Party's rank automatically reviewed (likely derank)
- Party flagged for fraud investigation if pattern suggests abuse
- Repeated offenders → permanent ban

**Frivolous Complaint Protection (Prevents Client Abuse):**
- AI evaluates each complaint for legitimacy
- Frivolous complaints rejected, count against CLIENT's reputation
- Three frivolous complaints in 6 months = client loses complaint privilege (must use formal dispute process)
- This prevents review extortion pattern (L-12) migrating to complaint system

**Complaint Categories:**
- Ghosting (no response >48 hours)
- Faulty work (deliverable doesn't meet scope)
- Wrong work (deliverable is incorrect)
- Scope manipulation
- Communication breakdown
- Other (with description)

**AI Warning Specificity Requirement:**
- AI warnings must be specific and actionable with deadline
- Example: "Complaint: Ghosting. Action required: respond to client within 24 hours. If no response, formal dispute initiated."
- Vague warnings like "we received a complaint" are FORBIDDEN — they create anxiety without action

**Rationale:** User proposed client complaint system with first warning + second complaint cancellation. AI Co-Founder refinements: (1) 3-stage progressive (not 2-stage) with forced cancellation at third complaint; (2) "Same type" requirement for escalation (fairer to parties — different issue = new first warning); (3) AI legitimacy check prevents client abuse; (4) frivolous complaint threshold prevents review extortion migration; (5) specific actionable warnings prevent anxiety. This addresses L-12 (Client Review Extortion — complaint weaponization prevented by legitimacy check), L-33 (Mid-Project Abandonment — ghosting complaints trigger formal process), L-29 (Infinite Revision Loops — faulty work complaints enable cancellation), and L-22 (Escrow Manipulation by Clients — complaint record protects party in disputes).
**Status:** Confirmed. Implementation Phase: Phase 2 (quest board + dispute resolution) + Phase 3 (AI legitimacy check — needs AI services). Cost/Effort: High (complaint workflow + AI legitimacy evaluation + escalation logic + rank integration).
